Credits

ALFinity stands on the shoulders of mathematicians who explored these shapes long before computers could draw them, and was built with the help of modern tools.

Mathematicians

Pierre Fatou (1878–1929)

French mathematician who, from 1917 on, studied what happens when a function is applied to complex numbers again and again – the groundwork for Julia sets.

Gaston Julia (1893–1978)

French mathematician whose prize-winning 1918 paper on the same question made him famous. The Julia sets carry his name – decades before anyone could see them on a screen.

Benoît B. Mandelbrot (1924–2010)

Mathematician who coined the word “fractal” in 1975 and showed how common fractal shapes are in nature. Working at IBM, he used computers to draw and study the set that now bears his name (1980) and made it world-famous.

Adrien Douady (1935–2006) and John H. Hubbard (born 1945)

They gave the Mandelbrot set its name, proved in 1982 that it is connected – all in one piece – and laid the foundations for understanding its structure.
